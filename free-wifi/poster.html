Free Wi-Fi,
Dear Price

The network with no password has no conscience.

A plain-language field guide to public & rogue Wi-Fi — the free connection that quietly reads over your shoulder.

01 / What it is

Anyone can stand up a Wi-Fi hotspot and name it whatever they like. The fella two seats over can call his phone “Airport_Free_WiFi” or “Gate_A12_Guest” — an evil twin — and your device, helpful soul, offers to join it. Now his phone, not the airport, sits between you and the internet.

02 / How the trap springs
1
Attacker broadcasts a hotspot with a trustworthy name.
2
You connect — no password, looks official.
3
Your traffic now routes through their device.
4
They read, or serve you a fake login to harvest.

→ They don’t need your password if they can borrow your logged-in session, or hand you a login page that looks exactly like your bank’s.

Where it hunts

The best hunting grounds are where people are bored, rushed and transient:

Airport Gates
Cafés
Hotels
Conferences
!
Do not bank at the gate No banking, no moving money, no logging into anything that matters over public Wi-Fi — airports and hotels most of all. You’re tired, you’re rushed, and that’s exactly the moment it costs you. If it touches money or a password, use mobile data instead.
03 / Why it’s dangerous
  • No password on a network means no protection on it either.
  • A fake login page looks identical to the real one — the padlock can lie.
  • Your phone auto-rejoins a named network it “knows” — including the twin.
  • Captive-portal pop-ups train you to accept dodgy screens without thinking.
04 / How you stay safe
  • Sensitive stuff? Use mobile data or your own hotspot, not public Wi-Fi.
  • Run a reputable VPN — it encrypts everything, even on a hostile network.
  • Bank from the bank’s app on cellular, never a browser on café Wi-Fi.
  • Turn off auto-join; “forget” public networks after you leave.
  • Keep two-factor on — it blunts a stolen password.
