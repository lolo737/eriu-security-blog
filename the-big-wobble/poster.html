THE BIG
WOBBLE

Nine agencies, one fortnight, and not all of it was hackers.

What actually happened to the CIA, FBI, NSA, FinCEN and friends in November 2026, what caused each one, and what it means for your office.

01 / What it is

There isn't one big attack here. There's a cluster: some confirmed intrusions, some intrusions reported by anonymous sources, and one outage that officials say was a software update gone sideways. Reporters, officials and even a few of the agencies disagree on the details, and most of it is still under investigation. Treat everything below as the picture on 28 November 2026, not the final word.

02 / How it works
1
A flaw turns up. Sometimes in software you run, sometimes in a portal or platform nobody on your team owns.
2
Machines find it first. AI-driven tools (ARTEX is the one named in reports) can scan and try entry points far quicker than a human team.
3
They come in the side door. An unclassified system, a contractor platform, a cloud upgrade. Not the vault.
4
You scramble. Systems come offline, every password gets reset, and the clean-up hurts almost as much as the intruder.

→ Even when no secrets leave the building, the outage, the doubt and the bill all arrive.

Where it hits

The same four weak spots turn up in every office, big or small:

Unpatched softwareA known flaw, left open a few weeks too long.
Third-party portalsThe side door that nobody in-house owns.
Rushed patchingThe fix that knocks the lights out.
Shared platformsOne system, several organisations' worth of exposure.
!
Patch fast, but patch with a safety netApply security updates quickly, because attackers' tools get faster every month. But test them on one machine first, take a backup or snapshot beforehand, and know how to roll back. The NSA shows that a rushed patch can cause an outage as surely as an attacker can.
03 / Why it's dangerous
  • Speed has flipped. Automated tools shrink the window between a flaw being found and being used.
  • The boring system is the target. 'Unclassified' or 'low value' systems often reveal more than you'd think, such as what you're watching and where you're thin.
  • Resets hurt too. Changing every password at once is the right instinct and still takes services down.
  • Not knowing is its own cost. Staff left in the dark, and customers left guessing, do real damage.
04 / How you stop it
  • Patch on a schedule, critical fixes within days, and test before you roll out to everyone.
  • Keep a rollback plan and a recent backup before any major update.
  • Inventory your third parties. Know every portal, supplier platform and cloud tool that touches your data, and who owns each.
  • Turn on MFA everywhere and keep an up-to-date list of admin accounts, so a reset is a plan and not a panic.
  • Write the incident plan before you need it: who decides, who tells staff, who tells customers.
