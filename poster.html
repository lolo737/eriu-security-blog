It looks like a mouse. It types like an intruder.

A plain-language field guide to BadUSB / HID-injection attacks — the trusted device that turns on you.

01 / What it is

A BadUSB device is a peripheral that lies about what it is. Plug it in and it quietly tells the computer it’s a keyboard — a Human Interface Device (HID). Machines trust keyboards completely: no install, no warning, no click required.

02 / How the attack works
1
Plugged in — looks like an ordinary stick, mouse or cable.
2
Announces itself to the system as a keyboard (HID).
3
The OS trusts it automatically — no prompt, no scan.
4
It “types” a burst of commands far faster than a human could.

→ In seconds it can open a foothold on the machine, then be pulled back out. No file is left behind for anyone to find.

03 / Why it’s dangerous
  • Nothing to scan — antivirus sees keystrokes, not a malicious file.
  • Works in seconds, then unplugs and walks out the door.
  • Beats “never download anything” training — it needs no download.
  • Every machine trusts its own keyboard, by default, forever.
04 / How you shut it down
  • USB device control — allow-list approved devices; block new HIDs by policy.
  • Lock or cap unused ports; fit port blockers on exposed machines.
  • Never plug in found or “free” sticks, cables or dongles.
  • Set endpoints to flag a newly-connected keyboard for approval.
  • Keep physical control of machines and disable autorun.
