    Post 2: Free Wi-Fi
    Free Wi-Fi isn't free. It's just paid for differently.

Sit in any café or airport lounge and your phone offers you a dozen networks. Anyone with a laptop can broadcast one called "Airport_Free_WiFi" and watch what people do next. In 2024 Australian police charged a man over exactly that: fake Wi-Fi networks at airports that harvested people's logins.

Three habits that cost nothing:
1. Treat public Wi-Fi as a stranger's network, because it is.
2. Don't log in to anything important on it without a VPN.
3. If a login page appears out of nowhere asking for your email and password, close it.

I've written this up in plain English as part of the Eriu security series: https://lolo737.github.io/eriu-security-blog/free-wifi/

    Post 3: Invoice redirection (construction)
    A supplier emails to say their bank details have changed. You update them. Three weeks later they ask why you haven't paid.

That's invoice-redirection fraud, and it's the main email scam in the figures. FraudSMART, run by BPFI, reported that Irish SMEs lost almost €10 million to email-related scams in 2023, about €12,000 each time on average, and most cases were this exact trick.

The fix isn't clever software. It's a rule: any change of bank details gets confirmed by phone, on a number you already had, before any money moves. Add a second person approving payments over a set amount, and two-step login on the email account that receives invoices.

Building firms deal with a lot of suppliers and subcontractors, which is exactly why this scam lands. If you'd like a one-page checklist for your office, message me and I'll send it over.

#Construction #FraudAwareness #Ireland
    Post 4: Three questions for a school bursar
    Three questions every school bursar should be able to answer about their IT, without phoning anyone:

1. If a teacher deleted last Tuesday's shared folder today, who can get it back, and how long would it take?
2. Who has administrator access to your systems, and would you know if that list changed?
3. When a member of staff leaves, how quickly does their access disappear?

If the honest answer to any of them is "I'm not sure", that's not a failing. It's a to-do. Schools hold sensitive information about children and families, and run on tight budgets and term-time deadlines, so predictable and boring is the goal.

Watch out: the rules on cold email

Marketing by email in Ireland is covered by GDPR and the ePrivacy Regulations (S.I. 336 of 2011). The Data Protection Commission's guidance says when consent is needed, what the exception for existing customers allows, and how people can object. I couldn't confirm from the DPC page how business addresses are treated, and I'm not a lawyer, so check before you build a list.

The safe way to start: email people who've asked to hear from you or you've met (for example at Chamber networking), use LinkedIn messages and phone calls for first contact, keep the list small, record where each address came from, and put who you are and an easy opt-out in every email.
