02

Back end

The shop front is no use if the fax doesn’t go out, or goes out and you can’t tell. This is what has to be true before anyone pays you to send paper to a government office.

Source: Telnyx’s own support page on German numbers, read 4 November 2026. Telnyx’s fax pricing and limits I haven’t seen; those are questions for them.
  • Answer

    Ask them, in writing, so you have the answers on file:

    • The price per page for outbound fax to Germany, Ireland, the UK, the US and Canada, and whether a failed or busy attempt is charged
    • Limits: faxes at once, pages per fax, file size, and whether new accounts start with lower limits
    • Which numbers you can send from, and what your account needs to be verified for fax
    • How delivery reports and failure reasons reach your server (webhooks), and how long Telnyx keeps the documents
    • A data-processing agreement for GDPR, since people upload tax and medical papers
    • Balance alerts or automatic top-up, so the balance never runs out silently and stops faxes

1. Price per page for outbound fax to DE, IE, GB, US and CA, and whether failed or busy attempts are billed.

  • Answer

    Telnyx’s page for a Berlin (030) number lists: an address in Berlin with a utility bill from the last three months, a signed German registration form, ID, and for a company a German company registration certificate and registration number.     3. Someone in Berlin holds the number in their name. Only if that is a real, written arrangement. Don’t put it on the site until it exists.

06

Facebook and Instagram ads

You’re handing the ad work to someone else, which is sensible. This section is the brief for them, and a few hard facts about what ads can and can’t do here.

The plain version: the tracking idea works, but the “bookmark this page” button doesn’t, and a €1 fax probably can’t pay for its own ad. Read a1 and a2 before spending anything.
  • Answer

    Two problems. Facebook and Instagram ads offer a fixed menu of buttons (such as Learn more or Sign up), not a “bookmark” button. And a bookmark is made inside the browser, so your site is never told. No internal code can count real bookmarks.

    What works instead:

    1. The ad button says Learn more, and the ad itself says “save this for when a letter asks for a fax”.
    2. The landing page has a friendly Save this page button that shows how to bookmark it or add it to the iPhone home screen, and records one click with the campaign code. That counts intent to save, not real bookmarks. Treat it as a soft signal.
    3. Optional: a “remind me by email” box. That is a real, countable thing, but you need consent wording and a German privacy page first.
  • Answer

    Audience: Berlin had 3,700,577 residents and Brandenburg 2,551,889 at the end of 2025, so about 6.25 million, not 7. Two per cent of that is about 125,000 people. Your own goal of roughly €400 a week is about 400 faxes a week, around 21,000 a year, well under 1% of the population. You don’t need 2%.

    What one €1 fax earns (my arithmetic, check the VAT with your accountant):

    • Price: €1.00. If that includes German VAT at 19%, about €0.16 is tax.
    • Stripe’s standard EEA card fee is, as far as I know, 1.5% + €0.25, about €0.27 on a €1 payment. Check Stripe’s current pricing.
    • That leaves roughly €0.57 before the Telnyx cost, which only Telnyx can tell you.

    So an ad only pays for itself if each paid fax costs you under about 50 cents to win. I wouldn’t expect paid ads to hit that for a one-off €1 purchase, and the €1.50 price with a €1 first-fax offer makes it tighter. I can’t promise that, because it depends on your real click costs, which a small test will reveal.

    What that means: run a small, capped test purely to measure cost per paid fax. Put most of your effort in search, guides and groups, where a customer costs nothing but time. Ads work better for the monthly plan or for repeat use, because one customer pays many times.

  • Answer

    What Meta lets you choose: place, age and gender are standard for ordinary ads, as far as I know. Meta has been removing many interest options and has special rules in the EU, and I couldn’t confirm whether any “unemployed” option exists. Your ad person will see what’s on offer in Ads Manager.

    What you can’t do: you can’t identify individual people as unemployed, and ad text may not say or imply “you are unemployed”. Meta’s rules forbid claiming to know a personal attribute such as money or job situation. Reach that group by situation instead: “The Jobcenter wants a fax?” is fine. “Are you unemployed?” is not.

    “Not technical” isn’t something you can target, so the ad and page do that job: plain words, a big button, the price shown before paying.

    My advice: don’t narrow too early. Start with all adults in Berlin and Brandenburg, run two or three different messages, and let the paid-fax numbers show which group actually buys. Narrowing before you have data usually makes ads dearer.
